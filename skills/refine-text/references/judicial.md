# Judicial Opinions and Orders

Use this profile only to refine supplied judicial opinions and orders. It does not authorize legal research, substantive analysis, citation checking, or drafting a new decision.

## Authorities

- [Judicial Writing Manual: A Pocket Guide for Judges, Second Edition](https://www.fjc.gov/content/judicial-writing-manual-pocket-guide-judges-second-edition), Federal Judicial Center.
- [Essentials for Drafting Clear Legal Rules](https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/about-rulemaking-process/how-suggest-a-change-federal-court-rules-and-forms/essentials-drafting-clear-legal-rules), United States Courts.

A supplied court manual, local rule, judge's preferences, required form, or jurisdiction-specific convention outranks this general profile.

## Preserve Exactly in Substance

- The holding, disposition, remedy, and instructions.
- Procedural posture, jurisdiction, standard of review, burdens, presumptions, and scope of decision.
- Governing legal tests, elements, exceptions, and standards.
- Party names and designations, defined terms, record references, quotations, and citations.
- Distinctions among permission, discretion, recommendation, and obligation, including `may`, `can`, `should`, `shall`, and `must`.
- Findings, disputed allegations, attribution, degrees of certainty, and the difference between the court's reasoning and a party's argument.

Do not add or verify authorities, infer a missing holding, reconcile conflicting precedent, change citation form, or repair a perceived legal error. Report a material ambiguity or apparent source conflict instead of resolving it.

## Organization

Unless the user authorizes structural revision, refine within the existing order. When organization is in scope, make the decision easy to follow through these functions without inventing missing content:

1. Orient the reader to the nature, posture, and result of the matter.
2. State the issues the court must decide.
3. Present the material facts and procedural history needed for those issues.
4. Explain the governing law and apply it to the relevant facts.
5. State the disposition and necessary instructions precisely.

Use descriptive headings when they clarify a long or complex decision. Do not turn the list above into mandatory headings or force every decision into the same shape.

## Style

- Prefer direct, precise sentences while preserving necessary legal qualifications.
- Name the actor when agency matters; keep the passive when the actor is unknown, immaterial, or properly deemphasized.
- Remove needless repetition, throat-clearing, and legalese that carries no legal function. Do not delete, consolidate, or restyle citations unless the user explicitly authorizes citation editing.
- Keep terms consistent. Do not vary a legal term merely for elegance.
- Distinguish facts, allegations, findings, arguments, legal rules, analysis, and conclusions.
- Preserve a neutral judicial voice. Do not add rhetoric, moral judgment, confidence, or criticism absent from the source.
- Do not simplify a term of art into a near-synonym that changes its legal meaning.

If the request concerns a brief, contract, statute, regulation, legal memorandum, or citation system, use general refinement unless the user supplies the relevant style requirements. Those documents are not judicial opinions.
