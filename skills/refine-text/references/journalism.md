# Journalism

Use this profile for supplied news reports, news releases, and other journalistic copy. It refines prose; it does not perform reporting, source verification, fact-checking, or newsgathering.

## Authorities and House Style

- For American news style, use the current [AP Stylebook](https://www.apstylebook.com/) when the applicable guidance is available.
- For British news style, a publication may use a supplied house guide; the free [Guardian Style Guide](https://www.theguardian.com/info/series/the-guardian-style-guide) is a useful published example.

AP and publication guides are house styles, not universal grammar. A supplied newsroom guide and the document's consistent established conventions outrank this profile. Do not claim complete AP compliance without access to the applicable current guidance.

## Fidelity

- Preserve quotations verbatim and keep their attribution.
- Preserve `alleged`, `reportedly`, `according to`, `said`, and similar language when it separates an allegation or source's characterization from established fact.
- Do not convert a charge into a conviction, an estimate into a count, a possibility into a certainty, or a source's conclusion into the reporter's voice.
- Preserve names, titles, dates, numbers, chronology, locations, and source relationships.
- Never add color, scenes, reactions, motives, anecdotes, eyewitness detail, background, or statistics absent from the source.
- Do not replace neutral attribution verbs with interpretive verbs such as `admitted`, `conceded`, `boasted`, or `insisted` unless the source supports that characterization.

## News Style

- Lead with the most newsworthy source-supported fact when structural revision is requested. Otherwise preserve the existing order.
- Use concrete nouns and strong factual verbs. Remove journalese, promotional wording, vague superlatives, and inflated claims.
- Keep paragraphs focused and readable without forcing every sentence or paragraph to the same length.
- Attribute disputed, evaluative, or non-obvious claims close to the claim.
- Separate news reporting from analysis, opinion, and advocacy. Preserve a clearly identified commentary genre rather than neutralizing it.
- Maintain consistent treatment of names, titles, organizations, numbers, dates, abbreviations, and geographic terms.
- Avoid formulaic transitions and summaries; let chronology and logical relationships carry the story.
- Preserve the distinction between a headline, standfirst, caption, quotation, and body copy.

Ask for or follow the publication's house guide when a choice about capitalization, punctuation, numerals, titles, datelines, or terminology would materially affect the result. Otherwise preserve the source's consistent convention.
