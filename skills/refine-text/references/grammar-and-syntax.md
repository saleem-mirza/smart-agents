# Grammar and Syntax

Use this reference when the edit turns on grammar, syntax, collocation, modality, dialect, or disputed usage. It is a shared foundation, not a genre or house style.

## Authority and Scope

Primary online reference: [Cambridge English Grammar Today](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/grammar/british-grammar/), a corpus-informed guide to spoken and written English, including standard and non-standard varieties.

Cambridge guidance informs grammatical decisions. The user's instructions, a supplied house guide, the document's established dialect, and genre-specific conventions still govern spelling, punctuation, capitalization, citations, and format.

## Checks

- Confirm that every finite clause has the required subject and verb and that dependent clauses attach clearly.
- Match subjects and verbs despite intervening nouns.
- Give pronouns and demonstratives one clear antecedent.
- Attach modifiers to the words they modify and place limiting words such as `only` precisely.
- Preserve the source timeline through consistent and logically related tense and aspect.
- Keep coordinated clauses, lists, comparisons, and headings parallel.
- Preserve distinctions among ability, permission, possibility, advice, and obligation in modal verbs.
- Check article, preposition, and verb-pattern choices in context rather than replacing them mechanically.
- Prefer natural collocations for the intended dialect and register.
- Repair sentence boundaries, unintended fragments, run-ons, and comma splices without flattening deliberate rhythm.

## Variation and Usage

- A regional or social variety is not an error merely because it differs from another standard.
- Preserve a grammatical variant when it is consistent with the document's dialect and voice.
- Do not enforce schoolroom prohibitions against split infinitives, sentence-ending prepositions, contractions, passive voice, or singular `they` without a contextual reason or binding style rule.
- When multiple forms are grammatical, prefer the form already used consistently in the document.
- Treat grammar, usage, and house style as different questions. Do not present a publication preference as a universal rule of English.

Never change a fact, technical distinction, quotation, defined term, or degree of certainty merely to make a sentence easier to parse.
